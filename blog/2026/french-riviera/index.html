"I can't survive my South FRA trip without Gemini."「没有 Gemini，我可能没法在这趟南法之旅中生存下来。」

On my June break, right after running the B.A.A. 10K in Boston, I took a trip to the South of France — the French Riviera / Côte d'Azur — before my France (FRA) visa expired. It was my second time in Europe; the last was the Netherlands 🇳🇱 in 2023.

The trip drove home one thing: AI assistants are quietly becoming an indispensable tool in everyday life. From four flight delays and lost luggage to ordering food, seeing a doctor, and cross-border accessibility, almost every step leaned on Google Gemini. This post walks through each leg of the trip and what Gemini actually helped with (including the bad advice it occasionally gave).

Not long ago, over lunch with Matt White  (CTO of the Linux Foundation & PyTorch Foundation), we were talking about how widely AI assistants have spread — a French grandmother had used Gemini to ask us for directions in Bellevue. I didn't expect it to be my own turn so soon: this time, it was me leaning on Gemini in the South of France.

Two apps carried this trip: Google Gemini was the workhorse throughout (queries, translation, emergencies, planning), and Rednote (Xiaohongshu) was my side reference for travel tips (local recommendations, pitfalls, photo spots). A fun discovery: in France, Google Search has no AI Mode, so I just used the Gemini app the whole time.

🎒 Before the Trip: eSIM, Plug Adapter, Cash

Before leaving, Gemini helped me knock out a few must-do pre-trip errands: eSIM data, the power adapter, and whether to exchange cash.

What Gemini helped with

📱 Data & SIM: eSIM (Airalo)

I used an Airalo eSIM with a 7-day / 5GB France plan — online the moment I landed, no hunting for a physical SIM, and data ready for maps, baggage tracking, dining, and train tickets throughout. Beforehand I had Gemini compare eSIM options (coverage, data, price) and walk me through purchase, install, and turning on data roaming after landing.

🔌 Power adapter (US → EU)

Gemini confirmed a US → EU plug adapter is a must, and that phone/laptop chargers are wide-voltage so an adapter alone is fine — only fixed-120V heating tools like hair dryers would need more.

💶 Exchange cash?

Its advice: cards work almost everywhere in Nice — just keep ~€50–100 in small notes for markets, tips, and coin-operated toilets, withdrawn from a local ATM rather than exchanged at the airport.

🚋 Getting Around: Flights, Luggage, City Trams & Regional Trains

The red-eye from Boston to Nice, hunting for luggage on arrival, city trams, and cross-city TER trains — this whole leg ran almost entirely on Gemini. I carry some baggage around European trains: back in 2023 I botched a metro transfer in Amsterdam (see my rant back then), so this time I had Gemini plan every leg in advance. Here are just the most representative moments.

What Gemini helped with

✈️ Four delays: a red-eye that became an all-nighter

A Delta red-eye direct from Boston (BOS) to Nice (NCE), planned to leave 8:00 PM and land 9:35 AM the next morning — but it kept slipping: 8:00 PM → 11:25 PM → 12:15 AM → 1:10 AM.

🛄 Lost luggage: 2+ hours at NCE

When my checked bag still hadn't appeared after two hours, Gemini clarified the whole process — where to find ground staff, how to file a lost-baggage claim, which receipts to keep. In the end, a mom on my flight noticed a handful of bags had been dropped on a belt in another corner of the terminal — that's where I found mine.

🚊 Airport to city: the €10 tourist ticket vs the €1.70 local price

The machines outside the terminal are "tourist-restricted" and only sell a €10 round-trip. Gemini's hack: Tram Line 2 is free for the first couple of stops between the airport and the city edge — board empty-handed, ride one stop to Grand Arénas, buy a €2 card + €1.70 single at that platform's machine, then hop back on to Jean Médecin downtown.

🎟️ T1 tram vs TER train

Gemini untangled the two systems: T1 (Tramway) is the in-city tram — buy at the platform machine and tap to validate on board (skipping it risks a €60+ fine); TER is SNCF's regional train for cross-city trips (Antibes, Cannes, Menton) — easiest via the SNCF Connect app e-ticket, or a paper ticket that must be stamped at the yellow validator before boarding.

🖼️ An Easter egg: the《Blind》sculpture at Port Vauban

Out of Antibes station, go left on flat ground to Port Vauban (don't go right, up the old town's cobbles). On a bench sits a bronze figure with a paint bucket over its head, hunched over a phone. Gemini identified it as Blind by French artist David David — a jab at modern "digital blindness": faceless, sealed off from the coast right in front of it, a public mirror for every head-down passerby. On a trip that leaned entirely on a phone AI, the irony wasn't lost on me.

📮 Little Quests: Postcards, the NICE Postmark, Handprints in Cannes

Beyond the sea, this trip had a few "little quests / side quests": mail postcards to friends and myself, make sure they carried a "NICE" postmark, and find a film star's handprint at the Palais des Festivals in Cannes. Gemini helped knock out these small-but-fiddly errands too.

What Gemini helped with

✉️ Postcards: getting the NICE postmark

There was a catch to my postmark goal: street boxes are machine-cancelled at a regional center, with no guarantee of a "NICE" mark. Gemini's safe play: Monday morning, before the train to Antibes, walk 3 flat minutes to La Poste NICE THIERS by the station and ask the counter to hand-cancel it — "Pouvez-vous tamponner à la main avec le cachet de Nice, s'il vous plaît ?" — and watch them stamp it. Done, and straight onto the train.

🎬 Hunting for handprints in Cannes

The ground around the Palais des Festivals red carpet is studded with the handprints of film masters — I specifically wanted Quentin Tarantino's. Gemini pinned it down: facing the red-carpet steps, head left toward the Esplanade Georges Pompidou tiles and look for the wild, looping "Q" signature with two palm prints between "Quentin" and "Tarantino."

Then came a fun myth-busting session — Is Brad Pitt's handprint in Cannes? What about Johnny Depp (Captain Jack)? And Jim Carrey (The Truman Show)? Gemini's answer, every time: no, their prints are all in Hollywood. The ones actually worth seeking out here: Tarantino (left of the carpet), Wong Kar-wai (WKW, by the main entrance), and Akira Kurosawa (AK, near the Palais).

🚑 When Things Went Wrong: Meds, the ER & Re-planning

Things still went sideways — one day I was biking by the sea along Nice's Promenade des Anglais, and then I fell. 😅 The self-care, buying meds, the ER visit, and re-planning the trip afterward all leaned on Gemini, step by step.

What Gemini helped with

Before the ER, Gemini triaged the situation: it identified whether the pharmacy's white spray could reduce the swelling, taught me the R.I.C.E. routine, and gave a clear escalation rule — can't bear weight or the toe looks deformed, get an X-ray. When I went in, it laid out the foreigner ER process (passport, pay by card first, ≈ €100–300 price-capped), wrote a French paragraph describing my injury for the triage nurse, and listed the "reimbursement trio" to collect before leaving — Facture Acquittée, Feuille de Soins, and the medical report + X-ray — since French public hospitals can't bill US insurance directly.

With the Donjoy boot on and no fracture, it then re-planned the rest of the trip: go to flat Monaco and Cannes; skip the hilltop cobbles of Èze and Saint-Paul-de-Vence; taxi over bus, switch to "Riviera vacation mode," and keep the Doliprane and Voltaren gel handy.

🍽️ Restaurants & Ordering

A tip: for popular spots, arrive about an hour early to queue. When ordering I often had no idea what drink to get, so I let Gemini recommend based on the menu and local habits. I also kept Gemini's French ordering/communication lines in Apple Notes to show the staff — super handy.

What Gemini helped with

📖 Decoding the menu at Le Cénac

Before dinner at Le Cénac, Gemini turned the menu into a cheat sheet: Escargots de Bourgogne (mop the garlic butter with baguette) and warm goat-cheese salad among Les Entrées, and Confit de canard as the main.

🍰 Ordering dessert from a blackboard photo

Faced with the blackboard dessert menu, I just photographed it and asked Gemini to order for me. Top pick: Fondant chocolat (€9) — hot molten chocolate with cold vanilla ice cream; runner-up: Café Gourmand (€9).

♿️ The Trip Home: Cross-3-Country, One Transfer Accessibility (WCHR)

The way home was more complex — three countries' airports (one transfer: NCE → LHR → SEA, changing at London Heathrow). The key was arranging wheelchair assistance for the whole route in advance. Aviation has standard IATA special-assistance codes, so I had Gemini clarify which to request and how to talk to ground staff:

  • WCHR (Wheelchair – Ramp): can manage stairs and walk to the seat, just can't walk long distances; needs a wheelchair to/from the gate/jet bridge.
  • WCHS (Wheelchair – Steps): can't manage stairs, but can walk to the seat in-cabin; needs a wheelchair to the aircraft door.
  • WCHC (Wheelchair – Cabin): needs a wheelchair the whole way, including to the seat and in-cabin.

Since there's only one transfer (at London Heathrow, LHR), the key is to request this assistance at booking/check-in and make sure the LHR connection doesn't break down — with just 1 hour, crossing borders (UK→US) and re-clearing transfer security, that's where it most easily goes wrong.

What Gemini helped with

It explained the WCHR / WCHS / WCHC differences and helped me pick which to request (I ended up on WCHR); generated English/French lines to request assistance at Nice check-in and the LHR transfer; flagged the tight 1-hour connection.

💬 Prompt
Where and when should I request this connection wheelchair assistance?
✦ Gemini

Since you've already checked in at Nice, don't wait until the gate — the safest is to find ground staff (or a help desk) in the Nice departures hall now. Nice Airport has dedicated passenger assistance; the earlier BA notes it in the system at Nice, the earlier Heathrow can have someone waiting at the jet bridge.

Find BA ground staff: return to the BA counter, or go to your gate, or look for an "Information" / "Service Plus" / "Accompagnement" desk. Show this French line:

"Bonjour, je me suis blessé à la jambe droite. Je viens de m'enregistrer pour mon vol. Comme j'ai une escale très courte d'une heure à Londres Heathrow, est-il possible d'ajouter une assistance handicapé / fauteuil roulant (Wheelchair Assistance) pour mon transfert à Londres ? Merci."
(Hi, I injured my right leg. I just checked in for my flight. Since I only have a very short 1-hour connection at London Heathrow, could you add wheelchair assistance for my London transfer? Thanks.)

If they enter it, they'll say "C'est fait" (done). To be safe, confirm the code — for a short-distance leg injury it's WCHS (can't walk far but can board) or WCHR.

💬 Prompt
After they say "C'est fait," how do I double-check in French that the code is really in the system?
✦ Gemini

To double-check the code with them in French:

"Merci. Est-ce que vous avez bien enregistré le code WCHR ou WCHS pour l'assistance à Londres ?"
(Thanks. Did you register the WCHR or WCHS code for the London assistance?)
"C'est juste pour être sûr que la demande de fauteuil roulant apparaisse bien dans le système à Heathrow."
(Just to be sure the wheelchair request shows in the Heathrow system.)

💡 They'll likely reply:

"Oui, c'est bien confirmé en WCHR / WCHS." (Yes, confirmed as WCHR / WCHS.) · "Tout est bon dans le système pour Londres." (Everything's set for London.)

Once you hear "Oui" or see them nod, your London wheelchair service is locked in.

💬 Prompt
All-BA through ticket, only 1 hour to connect at London — do I need to collect my checked bag? Is that enough time?
✦ Gemini

On a single British Airways through-ticket:

1. Checked luggage — no need to collect. It's tagged through to Seattle (SEA) at Nice check-in; during the 1-hour London connection the system transfers it automatically.

2. Terminal & transfer

  • Same terminal: both BA flights (Nice in, Seattle out) usually use Heathrow T5 — no terminal change.
  • Timeline: even in one terminal, 1 hour is right at Heathrow's "minimum connection time." You can't exit customs; follow the purple "Flight Connections" signs and re-clear UK transfer security.

🚨 Note (given your right-leg injury)

1 hour means walking, queuing, and re-screening. In your current state, walking it yourself will not make the flight.

At Nice check-in, hand over your booking code and passport and clearly request: (1) confirm the bag is tagged to SEA; (2) book LHR wheelchair assistance on the spot, telling them the connection is only 1 hour and you're injured. Then at London, a wheelchair agent meets you at the jet bridge and takes you via the Fast Track green lane past the security queue — the only way to make the Seattle flight in an hour.

💬 Prompt
Write me a line to ask the crew: I requested arrival wheelchair assistance — should I stay in my seat until they come onboard, or walk to the jet bridge myself?
✦ Gemini

"Hi, I have requested airport wheelchair assistance for my arrival. Should I remain in my seat until the wheelchair agent comes onboard to get me, or should I walk out to the jet bridge myself?"

💶 Airport tax refund (Détaxe)

Gemini also sorted my receipts for the airport tax refund: only goods taken home qualify (with a barcode refund form issued at purchase) — never dining, medical, or the Donjoy boot — and pointed me to the orange PABLO kiosk at Nice airport for the customs e-stamp.

One small observation from all the wheelchair assistance: the wheelchairs at the European airports — Nice (NCE) and London Heathrow (LHR) — were noticeably newer and in far better shape than the ones back at Seattle (SEA).

🚨 Safety: the Riviera's "pickpocket ecosystem"

"Watch out, pickpockets."

— a staff member openly warning the waiting crowd, in the NCE passport-control line right after we landed

The South of France is pickpocket territory, so before the trip I also had Gemini explain "why so many thieves" and "how safe Nice really is."

What Gemini helped with

Its explanation of the "why" was genuinely illuminating: the Riviera concentrates wealthy, relaxed tourists ("gold everywhere" to a thief); one coastal rail line plus the Italian border 40 minutes away makes the perfect getaway; penalties for non-violent theft are very light; and the ornate old façades are practically climbing walls for "balcony crews." In a line: theft here is high reward, low cost and risk — but these are opportunists who only pick the easiest targets.

As for Nice itself: generally safe, violent crime rare, but watch for pickpockets on the Promenade des Anglais and in the old town's narrow streets, never leave a bag on the beach while swimming, be careful around the central station at night, wear the bag in front, and politely decline the "sign a petition / help with your bag" scams.

🤔 Gemini Hallucinates Too

Of course, it isn't always right. One night at a four-star hotel, worried a thief might climb in from the balcony, I asked Gemini — and got a full, battle-ready "anti-balcony-intrusion" plan:

What Gemini helped with
💬 Prompt
Is balcony break-in theft common at Nice hotels? The front desk won't move my room — what do I do tonight?
✦ Gemini

It assured me balcony break-ins "aren't rare" on the Riviera, described the thieves' MOs in cinematic detail (2–5 AM entries, "balcony grabs" while you shower), and issued tonight's "iron rules": lock the balcony fully no matter the heat, scramble the suitcase lock, and — the highlight — wedge a chair hard against the balcony door with a glass upside-down on top as a noise barrier, turning the room into a "hard target."

…And the result? All four nights at the hotel were perfectly fine — nobody climbed the balcony. Maybe it was just too hot that week and the thieves were too lazy. In hindsight the "brace-the-door + upside-down-glass" plan was a bit much — AI is powerful, but it still needs common sense to keep it in check: it's the co-pilot, not the autopilot.

🇫🇷 A Few Differences from the US

A few everyday differences I noticed between the South of France and the US:

  • Greetings matter. Open with Bonjour when entering a shop or asking directions, and Merci when leaving or helped — I tacked a merci onto nearly every sentence.
  • No tipping culture — the bill is basically the final price.
  • Free tap/mineral water is standard at restaurants.

🌡️ Heat alert: I caught the Ironman cancellation.

While I was in the South of France, a historic heatwave swept Europe. The Alpes-Maritimes prefecture, citing an orange heat alert, outright cancelled the IRONMAN France Nice and IRONMAN 70.3 Nice races set for June 28, affecting ~4,500 athletes who'd already arrived on the Riviera. The World Meteorological Organization later confirmed France recorded its hottest day on record on June 24 (national average 30.0°C, with 43.8°C hit in Pulluau), with a top-level red alert issued for 58 departments, in a report titled
"Record-breaking heat spreads through Europe" (Sources: 220 Triathlon · Tri247 · WMO)

🎒 行前准备：eSIM、转换插头、现金

出发前，Gemini 帮忙搞定了几件"上路前必办"的小事：eSIM 上网、电源转换插头、以及要不要换现金。

Gemini 帮上了什么忙

📱 上网与电话卡：eSIM（Airalo）

这次用的是 Airalo 的 eSIM，装了一个 7 天 / 5GB 的法国流量包——落地即联网，省去到当地再找实体 SIM 卡的麻烦，也让后面查地图、查行李、订餐、买火车票全程有网可用。出发前先用 Gemini 对比了不同 eSIM 方案（覆盖国家、流量额度、价格），并搞清楚购买、安装与落地后启用数据漫游的步骤。

Gemini 确认了美标转欧标转换插头必须带；手机、笔记本充电器是宽电压，插上转换头就能用——只有吹风机这类固定 120V 的加热电器才需要额外操心。

💶 要不要换现金？

它的建议：尼斯刷卡几乎无处不在，只需备约 50–100 欧的小面额零钱用于市集、小费和投币洗手间，落地后在当地 ATM 取现即可，别在机场换汇。

从波士顿到尼斯的红眼航班、落地找行李、市内电车到跨城 TER 火车——这一路的交通几乎全程靠 Gemini。说到欧洲的火车我其实有心理阴影：2023 年在阿姆斯特丹地铁换乘闹过大乌龙（见当时的吐槽推文），所以这次每段都提前让它规划。下面只摘选几个最有代表性的片段。

Gemini 帮上了什么忙

✈️ 航班四次延误：红眼变通宵

Delta 从波士顿 (BOS) 直飞尼斯 (NCE) 的红眼航班，原计划 8:00 PM 起飞、次日 9:35 AM 抵达，却一再延误：8:00 PM → 11:25 PM → 12:15 AM → 1:10 AM 才起飞。

🚊 机场到市区：€10 游客票 vs €1.70 本地价

机场航站楼外的售票机被设了"游客限制"，只卖 €10 往返特票。Gemini 支的招：轻轨 2 号线从机场出发的前几站免费——空手上车坐 1 站到 Grand Arénas，在站台机器买 €2 实体卡 + €1.70 单程票，再上车直达市中心 Jean Médecin。

🎟️ T1 电车 vs TER 火车

Gemini 把两套系统讲明白了：T1（Tramway）是市内有轨电车，站台买票、上车"嘀"卡验票（漏验被查罚 60 欧起）；TER 是法国国铁区域火车，去昂蒂布、戛纳、芒通等跨城用，最省事是用官方 App SNCF Connect 买电子票，纸质票则上车前必须在黄色打票机打戳。

🖼️ 彩蛋：沃邦港的《盲目》雕塑

昂蒂布出站往左走平路就到沃邦港（别往右爬老城碎石坡）。港口长椅上坐着一座头扣油漆桶、低头玩手机的青铜雕塑。Gemini 认出这是法国当代艺术家 David David 的《盲目》（Blind）：讽刺现代人的"数字盲目"——本该欣赏眼前的海岸，却用一个"桶"把自己与真实世界隔绝。在这趟"全程离不开手机 AI"的旅行里，这份反讽格外应景。

📮 小心愿：寄明信片、盖尼斯邮戳、戛纳找手印

除了看海，这趟还有几个"小心愿 / side quest"：给自己和朋友寄明信片、一定要盖上"尼斯（NICE）"字样的邮戳、以及在戛纳影节宫找电影明星的手印。这些琐碎但要紧的小事，Gemini 也一路帮着搞定。

Gemini 帮上了什么忙

✉️ 寄明信片：一定要盖尼斯邮戳

盖"NICE"邮戳这个目标有个坑：投街边黄色邮筒会被送到区域处理中心机械盖戳，无法保证盖上"NICE"字样。Gemini 给的最稳办法：周一上午去昂蒂布之前，先走 3 分钟平路到火车站旁的 La Poste NICE THIERS，把明信片递给柜台说 "Pouvez-vous tamponner à la main avec le cachet de Nice, s'il vous plaît ?"（请人工盖尼斯邮戳），看着盖完当面投递——搞定，无缝上火车。

🎬 戛纳找手印

戛纳影节宫红毯周边的地面上，嵌着许多电影大师的手印——特意想找昆汀·塔伦蒂诺的那一枚。Gemini 给出了精确定位：面向红毯台阶往左手边走，在蓬皮杜大广场（Esplanade Georges Pompidou）的地砖里找那个狂放连笔的 "Q" 字签名——上方带圈圈的 "Quentin"、下方长长的 "Tarantino"，中间一双掌纹。

🚑 意外处理：药品、就医与行程调整

♿️ 回程安排：跨三国、一次转机的无障碍通道（WCHR）

回程比来时更复杂——这趟要跨三个国家的机场（一次转机：NCE → LHR → SEA，在伦敦希思罗 LHR 转机）。最关键的一件事，是提前把全程的无障碍与轮椅协助（wheelchair assistance）安排妥当。航空业有一套标准的 IATA 特殊协助代码，于是让 Gemini 帮忙厘清该申请哪一种，以及在每一段转机怎么和地勤沟通：

  • WCHR（Wheelchair – Ramp）：能自己上下台阶、也能走到座位，只是无法长距离步行，需要轮椅接送往返登机口／廊桥。
  • WCHS（Wheelchair – Steps）：无法自行上下台阶，但能在客舱内走到座位，需要轮椅送到舱门。
  • WCHC（Wheelchair – Cabin）：全程都需要轮椅，包括协助送到座位、在客舱内也需要帮助。

因为全程只有一次转机（在伦敦希思罗 LHR），关键是把这段轮椅协助在订票／值机时就提前 request 好，并确保 LHR 转机的衔接不掉链子——只有 1 小时、又要跨国（英→美）重过转机安检，最容易出问题。

Gemini 帮上了什么忙

讲清 WCHR / WCHS / WCHC 的区别，帮忙判断该申请哪一种（我最后用的是 WCHR）；生成在尼斯值机和 LHR 转机时向地勤请求轮椅协助的英语／法语话术；提醒 1 小时短转机的衔接注意事项。

🇫🇷 南法与美国的几点不同

这趟旅程里，也注意到南法和美国的一些日常差异：

  • 问候语很重要。进店、问路先说一句 Bonjour（你好），离开或收到帮助后说 Merci（谢谢）——几乎每句话都会顺带加一句 merci。
  • 没有小费文化（no tipping culture），账单基本就是最终价格。
  • 餐厅普遍免费提供矿泉水／饮用水。
