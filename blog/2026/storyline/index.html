A Storyline Threading the Two Coasts

In June 2026, ten years after graduation, I used a stretch of time off to return to Boston — this time to run the B.A.A. 10K, and then to give the rest of the trip to the island of Nantucket, Massachusetts, a place I had wanted to see since my student days.

Ten years ago I moved from the Greater Boston Area, MA to the Greater Seattle Area, WA. Coming back to Massachusetts a decade later, the storyline seems to close a loop: just as those quoted passages say, Seattle's Starbucks and Massachusetts's Nantucket are bound together by a line drawn through Moby-Dick — Starbucks takes its name from Starbuck, the coffee-loving first mate, and the place where Moby-Dick weighs anchor is Nantucket.

A Worldview: Storyline-Driven Plot Progression and Completion

Unlike most of my peers, I don't travel out of town much. Most of the time it's for a conference, or together with friends; and there's a very personal reason too — I dislike long-distance travel, so I mostly stay tucked away in the PNW.

But I've always held a worldview: storyline-driven plot progression and a pursuit of completion — and that includes storyline-driven travel. For me a trip isn't about checking off a coordinate; it's about pushing a line whose foreshadowing was laid long ago toward the scene it was meant to reach, and adding a note of "completion" to a chapter left hanging for years. Nantucket, to me, was exactly such a plot node waiting to be lit up.

My first time in Seattle was 2014. It was a solo trip — I traveled through several West Coast cities on my own, including SF and LA, and Seattle was the last stop, because a college senior of mine was interning at MSR (Microsoft Research) and hosted me. I never imagined that after graduation I'd work full time in this city, all the way to now.

Looking back, that 2014 West Coast trip was really the opening chapter of this storyline; and this 2026 trip to Massachusetts is the scene where it circles back to its origin and pushes forward again. From the Pacific Northwest to the waters south of Cape Cod, from the first Starbucks at Pike Place to the little island where Moby-Dick weighs anchor — the threads were always there. It just took me ten years to draw them into a single route.

📍 Nantucket, MA — the storyline is complete here. 🏝️

一条串起东西海岸的故事线

2026 年 6 月，毕业十年之后，我利用一段休假时间重新回到波士顿——这次是为了跑 B.A.A. 10K，也顺势把后面的时间留给了马萨诸塞州的南太基岛，那个读书时期就想去看一看的地方。

十年前，我从大波士顿地区（Greater Boston Area, MA）搬到了大西雅图地区（Greater Seattle Area, WA）。十年之后再回麻省，故事线仿佛在此刻闭合了一圈：就像前面摘抄的文字里写的那样，西雅图的星巴克，和麻省的南太基，有着一条紧密的、由《白鲸》串起的线——星巴克之名取自《白鲸记》里那位嗜咖啡的大副 Starbuck，而 Moby Dick 的开卷之地，正是南太基。

一个世界观：故事线推动的剧情发展和完成度

和大部分同龄人不同，我出 town 出行不多。大部分时候是因为会议需要，或者是和朋友一起；再加上一个很个人的原因——我讨厌长途旅行，所以大多数时间都窝在 PNW 地区。

但我一直有一个世界观：故事线推动的剧情发展和完成度尝试。这里面也包括故事线推动的旅行。一次出行，对我来说不是为了打卡一个坐标，而是为了把某一条早已埋下伏笔的线，推向它应该到达的下一幕、把某个悬置多年的章节补上一个"完成度"。南太基之于我，就是这样一处等待被点亮的剧情节点。

第一次到西雅图是 2014 年。那是一趟 solo trip，我一个人跑了西海岸的几座大城市，包括 SF、LA，而 Seattle 是最后一站——因为那时有一位本科学长在 MSR（Microsoft Research）intern，他接待了我。当时完全没有想到，毕业以后我会在这座城市 full time，一直待到现在。

如今回望，那趟 2014 年的西海岸之旅，其实就是这条故事线最初的开篇；而 2026 年的这次麻省之行，是它兜回原点、又向前推进的一幕。从太平洋西北到大西洋鳕岬之南，从派克市场的第一家星巴克到 Moby Dick 起锚的小岛——线索一直都在，只是我花了十年，才把它们连成同一条航线。

📍 Nantucket, MA，故事线在这里完整。🏝️
